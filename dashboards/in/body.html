Chart notes

  1. India manufacturing GVA growth: MoSPI’s published growth rates, % y/y. Annual: the 2022-23 base from FY24 (solid) and the 2011-12 base for every year MoSPI published it, to FY26 (dashed), latest estimate of each year. The two bases are not comparable, so their lines are not joined. FY13 and FY14 are left out: MoSPI’s data hold conflicting values for those years. FYTD is the fiscal year so far, April to June 2026, the only quarter published. Quarterly: 2022-23 base, from April–June 2023.
  2. India manufacturing output and value added: annual: MoSPI’s published annual growth for manufacturing GVA (real) and the manufacturing IIP, 2011-12 base to FY23 and 2022-23 base from FY24, not joined across the change of base. FYTD: GVA for April–June 2026 as published, IIP calculated for the same months, the average index for April–June 2026 against April–June 2025. Quarterly: the IIP is the plain average of MoSPI’s published monthly y/y over the quarter.
  3. India IIP: MoSPI: MoSPI’s published y/y on the 2022-23 base, from April 2023. For April 2023 to March 2024 MoSPI compares each month with 2022-23 months normalised to 100, so those rates equal the index minus 100. Spliced: the 2011-12 base index divided by MoSPI’s linking factor (general 1.3834, manufacturing 1.3700) to March 2023, the 2022-23 base index from April 2023, y/y calculated from the linked index.
  4. India IIP top contributors: the five parts of the general IIP (2022-23 base) with the largest positive and negative contributions to general IIP y/y, averaged over the latest 3 or 6 months. The 30 parts are the most detailed level MoSPI publishes: the 23 manufacturing divisions (NIC 2-digit), the three mining groups (fuel, metallic and non-metallic minerals), electricity, gas supply, water supply, and sewerage and waste. Contribution = the part’s weight (MoSPI’s 2022-23 weights, %) × the change in its index over 12 months ÷ the general index a year earlier. y/y is MoSPI’s published rate for the latest month.
  5. India IIP by industry: 2022-23 base. 3mma: the plain average of MoSPI’s published y/y for the latest three months. YTD: the calendar year to date, the average index from January to the latest month against the same months a year earlier. Electronics: electronics and electrical equipment, NIC divisions 26 and 27. Non-electronics: the other 21 manufacturing divisions. Other industries: mining, electricity and gas, and water supply, sewerage and waste, the parts of the general IIP outside manufacturing. Each group index is the weighted average of MoSPI’s published indices at MoSPI’s 2022-23 weights (electronics 5.260, non-electronics 70.805, mining 11.053, electricity and gas 10.865, water and waste 2.020, out of 100), and its y/y is calculated.
  6. India electronics and non-electronics manufacturing IIP: the groups in the note above, % y/y, calculated, from April 2024, the first month with a 2022-23 base year earlier.
  7. Contributions to India IIP growth: each group’s weight × the change in its index over 12 months ÷ the general index a year earlier, MoSPI’s 2022-23 weights, groups as in the note on India IIP by industry. The line is MoSPI’s published general IIP y/y. The bars add up to it within 0.1pp.
  8. India IIP by use-based category: 2022-23 base, 3mma and YTD as in the note on India IIP by industry.
  9. Contributions to India IIP growth by use-based category: calculated as the contributions above, with MoSPI’s 2022-23 use-based weights: primary goods 31.136, intermediate goods 22.416, consumer non-durables 16.147, consumer durables 11.311, infrastructure and construction goods 10.908, capital goods 8.082. The bars add up to the published general IIP y/y within 0.11pp.
  10. India manufacturing share of GVA: manufacturing GVA at current prices divided by GDP or total GVA at current prices, MoSPI’s levels, latest estimate of each year, calculated on each base separately: 2011-12 base (dashed) and 2022-23 base (solid).
  11. India mfg producer and wholesale prices: the output Producer Price Index and the Wholesale Price Index for manufactured products, Office of the Economic Adviser, % y/y calculated from the published indices. Output PPI: 2022-23 base, indices from April 2023, so y/y from April 2024. WPI: linked, the 2011-12 base index divided by the office’s published linking factor for manufactured products (1.44) to March 2023, the 2022-23 base index from April 2023, y/y from April 2013. PPI ex-refined petroleum: the output PPI for manufactured products less coke and refined petroleum products, calculated from the published indices and weights. The output PPI counts refined petroleum as manufacturing, the WPI puts it under fuel and power. The two also use different weights.
  12. India mfg PPI top contributors: the five of the 74 product categories of manufactured products in the output PPI (2022-23 base), the most detailed level the office publishes, with the largest positive and negative contributions to manufactured products PPI y/y, averaged over the latest 3 or 6 months. Contribution = the category’s weight × the change in its index over 12 months ÷ (the weight of manufactured products × its index a year earlier). Weight is the category’s share of manufactured products, %. y/y is calculated from the published indices for the latest month.
  13. India PPI by group: output PPI, headline (all commodities) and 2022-23 base, the 23 groups of manufactured products (NIC 2-digit divisions). The office publishes indices only, so y/y is calculated from them. 3mma: the plain average of the latest three months’ y/y. YTD: the calendar year to date, the average index from January to the latest month against the same months a year earlier. Ex-refined petroleum: manufactured products less coke and refined petroleum products, at the published weights. Electricity: the output PPI’s electricity group. Rest of PPI: all commodities less manufactured products and electricity, that is agriculture, forestry and fishing, and mining, at the published weights.
  14. India manufacturing capacity utilisation: RBI, Order Books, Inventories and Capacity Utilisation Survey (OBICUS), quarterly, as published and seasonally adjusted by the RBI.

Consumer price index

India’s Consumer Price Index is compiled monthly by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) for rural areas, urban areas and the two combined. The combined index is the headline and the Reserve Bank of India’s inflation target. This page shows the all-India general index for all three.

One series from 2011

MoSPI moved the CPI to a 2024 base in February 2026, starting with January 2026 data. The new series covers January 2025 onwards. For earlier years MoSPI publishes a back series on the 2024 base: the old 2012-base general index multiplied by a linking factor, set so that the two series have the same geometric-mean average over 2025 (0.5267 combined, 0.5222 rural, 0.5320 urban). Its level is published to one decimal. The back series starts in January 2013.

For January 2011 to December 2012, Asia Decoded links MoSPI’s 2010-base general index to the back series the same way: the ratio of the two indices’ geometric-mean averages over 2013, the first year they overlap (0.4379 combined, 0.4317 rural, 0.4456 urban). The result is one index per sector, 2024 = 100, from January 2011.

The back series exists only for the general index, all-India, groups, items and states start in January 2025.

Year-on-year rates

Chart 8 calculates y/y from the spliced index, from January 2012. Because each link matches annual averages rather than each month, and the back series is rounded, these differ from the rates MoSPI published at the time: by 0.1pp on average, and most in 2013 and 2025, the years either side of a join. From January 2026, when the index is MoSPI’s own 2024-base series, they match MoSPI’s published rates. The headline figures and opening sentence use MoSPI’s published rates.

Core 1 and core 2

MoSPI does not publish a core measure. These two follow the definitions in the Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Report (April 2026, Box IV.2 and Table IV.1):

  • Core 1 (CPI excluding food and fuel). 2024 base: excluding food and beverages (division 01), electricity, gas and other fuels (group 04.5) and fuels and lubricants for personal transport (class 07.2.2: petrol, diesel, CNG), weight 53.0%. 2012 base: excluding the food and beverages and fuel and light groups and the items petrol for vehicle and diesel for vehicle, weight 45.0%.
  • Core 2 (CPI excluding food, fuel and precious metals). Core 1 also excluding gold and silver: gold/diamond/platinum jewellery and silver jewellery on the 2024 base (weight 52.1%), gold and silver on the 2012 base (weight 43.8%).

On the 2024 base each core index is the weighted average of MoSPI’s published item indices over the items kept (MoSPI’s CPI is a Young index, so this is exact, the same weights reproduce the published headline within 0.01 points). On the 2012 base, whose all-India indices MoSPI compiled from state indices, it is derived as the RBI does: the residual of the published headline index after removing the excluded groups and items at their weights. The RBI publishes the two bases side by side, with a break at January 2026. Asia Decoded instead links them, as MoSPI links its headline back series: the 2012-base core index is multiplied by the ratio of the two indices’ geometric-mean averages over 2025, the year both bases cover. Y/y is calculated from the linked index, from January 2015.

Rates for 2025 compare the new basket with the old basket a year earlier. Over the year they average the same as the RBI’s old-series rates (4.4% core 1, 3.1% core 2), but month by month they differ: by up to 0.9pp for core 1, mostly because gold is priced as jewellery in the new series and as bullion in the old, and by up to 0.3pp for core 2. Other changes inside core, such as rural housing entering the new series, also affect the comparison across the link. MoSPI published no 2012-base item indices for March–May 2020 (COVID-19), so core is missing for those months and y/y for March–May 2020 and 2021.

Long-term series

The two long-term charts show the IMF’s year-on-year rates for India’s all-items CPI, monthly and annual, from its Consumer Price Index dataset (formerly International Financial Statistics, series IND.CPI._T.YOY_PCH_PA_PT.M and .A). The annual rate is the change in the annual average. The IMF joins India’s indices at overlaps and does not publish where. Compared with MoSPI’s data, the IMF series matches the 2024-base combined CPI from January 2025 and the 2012-base combined CPI for 2013–2024, earlier years appear to be the CPI for Industrial Workers. The IMF series runs a month or two behind MoSPI.

Releases and revisions

MoSPI publishes the CPI on the 12th of the following month (the next working day if the 12th is a holiday). The latest month is provisional and is final in the next release.

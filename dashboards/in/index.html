Chart notes

  1. Chart 1, India inflation: output PPI and combined CPI (chart 8), % y/y. The output PPI starts in April 2023, before then it is extended back with the Wholesale Price Index (2011-12 base, all commodities): the two are linked by the ratio of their geometric-mean averages over 2024-25, the year and method the Office of the Economic Adviser uses for its own WPI linking factor (the same calculation on the WPI reproduces its published factor, 1.53), and y/y is calculated from the linked index (Asia Decoded calculations). Before April 2023 the line is therefore WPI inflation, which includes taxes and is weighted differently, and rates for April 2023 to March 2024 compare the output PPI with the WPI a year earlier. Producer prices cover goods only, with a large weight on industrial inputs, fuels and metals, while the CPI is weighted by household spending and includes services.
  2. Chart 2, India consumer inflation: a replication of the Reserve Bank of India’s Chart IV.1 (Monetary Policy Report, April 2026), without the target and tolerance band, extended back to January 2015 (the RBI’s chart starts in February 2024). Headline, food and beverages, fuel and CPI excluding food and fuel (core 1), RBI definitions, % y/y. Each rate compares prices on one basket only: on the 2012 base up to December 2025 and on the 2024 base from January 2026, joined as the RBI joins them, with no splicing. The dashed line marks the break between the two series, which, as the RBI notes, are not strictly comparable. Fuel on the 2012 base is the fuel and light group plus petrol and diesel for vehicles, on the 2024 base electricity, gas and other fuels plus fuels for personal transport (including CNG), weighted together with MoSPI’s weights. Headline and food and beverages are calculated from MoSPI’s indices, so they can differ from MoSPI’s published rates by 0.1pp from rounding. Asia Decoded calculations.
  3. Chart 3, India core inflation by CPI base: % y/y, calculated by Asia Decoded on each base separately, with no splicing, following the RBI’s definitions. Core 1 is CPI excluding food and fuel: food and beverages, and fuel (on the 2024 base, electricity, gas and other fuels plus petrol, diesel and CNG for vehicles, on the 2012 base, the fuel and light group plus petrol and diesel for vehicles). Core 2 is CPI excluding food, fuel and precious metals: core 1 also excluding gold and silver (on the 2024 base, gold/diamond/platinum jewellery and silver jewellery, on the 2012 base, gold and silver). Each rate compares prices on one basket only, as the RBI presents its core series. 2012 base, to December 2025: as the RBI derives it, the residual of MoSPI’s published headline index after removing the food and beverages and fuel and light groups and the petrol and diesel items (and, for core 2, gold and silver), with MoSPI’s 2012 weights (core 1 weight 45.0%, core 2 43.8%). 2024 base, from January 2026, the first month with a 2024-base year earlier: the weighted average of MoSPI’s 358 published item indices (all-India, combined) over the items kept, with MoSPI’s published item weights, excluding food and beverages (division 01), electricity, gas and other fuels (group 04.5) and petrol, diesel and CNG for vehicles (class 07.2.2), and for core 2 gold and silver jewellery (core 1 weight 53.0%, core 2 52.1%). The 2012-base rates reproduce the RBI’s published 2025 figures, and the 2024-base ones its 2026 figures, to one decimal. MoSPI published no 2012-base item indices for March–May 2020, for those months, petrol, diesel, gold and silver are imputed by Asia Decoded: the February 2020 index moved with the item’s monthly average market price (petrol and diesel: PPAC retail selling prices, average of Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai and Kolkata, gold and silver: RBI, Mumbai prices), benchmarked to MoSPI’s June 2020 index.
  4. Chart 4, India consumer inflation contribution: contributions, in percentage points, of food and beverages, fuel and CPI excluding food and fuel (RBI definitions, as in chart 3) to combined CPI y/y from chart 8, Asia Decoded calculations. Each is the component’s weight times the change in its published index over 12 months, divided by the headline index a year earlier, CPI excluding food and fuel is the residual, so the three add up to CPI y/y. To December 2024 they use MoSPI’s 2012-base indices and weights (food and beverages 45.9%, fuel 9.2%, the fuel and light group plus petrol and diesel for vehicles), from January 2026, the 2024-base ones (food and beverages 36.8%, fuel 10.2%, electricity, gas and other fuels plus fuels and lubricants for personal transport). In 2025 each year-on-year comparison spans the two baskets, so the series is chain-linked: each component’s contribution is split at the 2025 link (annual average) into its change on the old basket at 2012 weights and its change on the new basket at 2024 weights, the method used for annually chain-linked indices such as the euro area HICP. Comparability: the 2024 revision changed what each component contains, not just the weights: cooked meals and snacks bought out moved from food and beverages to restaurants, free PDS rice, wheat and pulses left the index, gold and silver moved from bullion to jewellery prices, and fuel now includes CNG. Contributions for 2025 therefore mix the old and new definitions, and partly reflect the reclassification rather than price changes. For this reason the RBI does not link the two series: it shows the 2012 base to December 2025 and the 2024 base from January 2026 side by side (Monetary Policy Report, April 2026, Chart IV.3), which gives different 2025 figures (for example, October 2025: CPI 0.25%, food and beverages −1.82pp, against 0.04% and −1.74pp here). Before 2025, CPI excluding food and fuel also absorbs the difference between chart 8’s spliced headline and the 2012-base headline (up to 0.14pp, from rounding in MoSPI’s back series). For March–May 2020, when MoSPI published no 2012-base item indices, petrol and diesel are imputed from market prices as in chart 3.
  5. Chart 5, India goods and services CPI: combined CPI (2024 base) split into goods and services, % y/y, Asia Decoded calculations, as MoSPI does not publish the split. Each is the weighted average of MoSPI’s published item indices with MoSPI’s item weights, the way MoSPI builds the headline, so the two together reproduce the published index. Items are classified by their COICOP 2018 type, which MoSPI marks on each subclass in its weights (non-durable, semi-durable and durable goods, or services), ten subclasses without a mark are typed from COICOP 2018 (for example, mobile phone charges and higher education tuition as services, mobile handsets and tobacco as goods). Goods weigh 70.5% and services 29.5%, restaurant meals and rent count as services. The RBI publishes its own estimates of the split, whose classification is not published and differs (Monetary Policy Report, April 2026). From January 2026, the first month with a 2024-base year earlier.

Consumer price index

India’s Consumer Price Index is compiled monthly by the Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) for rural areas, urban areas and the two combined. The combined index is the headline and the Reserve Bank of India’s inflation target. This page shows the all-India general index for all three.

One series from 2011

MoSPI moved the CPI to a 2024 base in February 2026, starting with January 2026 data. The new series covers January 2025 onwards. For earlier years MoSPI publishes a back series on the 2024 base: the old 2012-base general index multiplied by a linking factor, set so that the two series have the same geometric-mean average over 2025 (0.5267 combined, 0.5222 rural, 0.5320 urban). Its level is published to one decimal. The back series starts in January 2013.

For January 2011 to December 2012, Asia Decoded links MoSPI’s 2010-base general index to the back series the same way: the ratio of the two indices’ geometric-mean averages over 2013, the first year they overlap (0.4379 combined, 0.4317 rural, 0.4456 urban). The result is one index per sector, 2024 = 100, from January 2011.

The back series exists only for the general index, all-India, groups, items and states start in January 2025.

Year-on-year rates

Chart 8 calculates y/y from the spliced index, from January 2012. Because each link matches annual averages rather than each month, and the back series is rounded, these differ from the rates MoSPI published at the time: by 0.1pp on average, and most in 2013 and 2025, the years either side of a join. From January 2026, when the index is MoSPI’s own 2024-base series, they match MoSPI’s published rates. The headline figures and opening sentence use MoSPI’s published rates.

Core 1 and core 2

MoSPI does not publish a core measure. These two follow the definitions in the Reserve Bank of India’s Monetary Policy Report (April 2026, Box IV.2 and Table IV.1):

  • Core 1 (CPI excluding food and fuel). 2024 base: excluding food and beverages (division 01), electricity, gas and other fuels (group 04.5) and fuels and lubricants for personal transport (class 07.2.2: petrol, diesel, CNG), weight 53.0%. 2012 base: excluding the food and beverages and fuel and light groups and the items petrol for vehicle and diesel for vehicle, weight 45.0%.
  • Core 2 (CPI excluding food, fuel and precious metals). Core 1 also excluding gold and silver: gold/diamond/platinum jewellery and silver jewellery on the 2024 base (weight 52.1%), gold and silver on the 2012 base (weight 43.8%).

On the 2024 base each core index is the weighted average of MoSPI’s published item indices over the items kept (MoSPI’s CPI is a Young index, so this is exact, the same weights reproduce the published headline within 0.01 points). On the 2012 base, whose all-India indices MoSPI compiled from state indices, it is derived as the RBI does: the residual of the published headline index after removing the excluded groups and items at their weights. The RBI publishes the two bases side by side, with a break at January 2026. Asia Decoded instead links them, as MoSPI links its headline back series: the 2012-base core index is multiplied by the ratio of the two indices’ geometric-mean averages over 2025, the year both bases cover. Y/y is calculated from the linked index, from January 2015.

Rates for 2025 compare the new basket with the old basket a year earlier. Over the year they average the same as the RBI’s old-series rates (4.4% core 1, 3.1% core 2), but month by month they differ: by up to 0.9pp for core 1, mostly because gold is priced as jewellery in the new series and as bullion in the old, and by up to 0.3pp for core 2. Other changes inside core, such as rural housing entering the new series, also affect the comparison across the link. MoSPI published no 2012-base item indices for March–May 2020 (COVID-19), so core is missing for those months and y/y for March–May 2020 and 2021.

Long-term series

The two long-term charts show the IMF’s year-on-year rates for India’s all-items CPI, monthly and annual, from its Consumer Price Index dataset (formerly International Financial Statistics, series IND.CPI._T.YOY_PCH_PA_PT.M and .A). The annual rate is the change in the annual average. The IMF joins India’s indices at overlaps and does not publish where. Compared with MoSPI’s data, the IMF series matches the 2024-base combined CPI from January 2025 and the 2012-base combined CPI for 2013–2024, earlier years appear to be the CPI for Industrial Workers. The IMF series runs a month or two behind MoSPI.

Releases and revisions

MoSPI publishes the CPI on the 12th of the following month (the next working day if the 12th is a holiday). The latest month is provisional and is final in the next release.

Data

All figures are as published by MoSPI, none is calculated. MoSPI API (api.mospi.gov.in): 2024-base series and back series (getCPIData), and 2012-base rates for 2025 (getCPIIndex). Long-term series: IMF data portal (api.imf.org), dataset IMF.STA:CPI. Built by the AsiaDecoded/asia-vitals pipeline (data/in/cpi/in_cpi.csv), which checks for new data twice a day.

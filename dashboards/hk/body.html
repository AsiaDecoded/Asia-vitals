Chart notes

  1. All figures are as published by the Census and Statistics Department (C&SD); none is calculated. Indices are on the October 2019 – September 2020 = 100 base. Rates are C&SD’s, computed by C&SD from indices with more than one decimal; a published 0.0 means a change of less than 0.05%.
  2. Chart 1, Hong Kong consumer inflation: CPI from October 1981, when its y/y series starts, and the underlying CPI, which nets out the effects of the Government’s one-off relief measures (rates concessions, public housing rent waivers, electricity charges subsidies and similar). The underlying series starts in 2007. It is C&SD’s own measure and is shown on this page only; it is not the CPI excluding food and energy on the India and Singapore pages, which C&SD does not publish. CPI less food and non-alcoholic beverages and energy (calculated, from October 2020) is the core measure used across the Asia dashboards; see Methodology.
  3. Chart 2, Hong Kong CPI momentum: C&SD’s average monthly rate of change in the seasonally adjusted index over the latest three months, for the index chosen above (CPI(A), (B) and (C) from 1981, CPI from 1992). The underlying measure (dashed) is published for the latest 36 months only. C&SD revises seasonally adjusted figures as more data arrive; the page shows the latest vintage.
  4. Table, Hong Kong consumer inflation: y/y for the latest 13 months by commodity/service section, with selected groups. Weights are C&SD’s 2025 expenditure weights for the CPI (used from April 2026), in % of CPI. Each row is coloured on its own range, from green at its lowest rate in the window to red at its highest.
  5. Table and chart, contributions: the contributions of each section to CPI y/y, in percentage points, as C&SD publishes them in Table 3.05 of each month’s Monthly Report on the CPI (food split into meals out and takeaway food, and basic food). Each month is the figure in that month’s report; the web reports start with the June 2023 issue. The published parts are rounded to one decimal, so they can differ from the published y/y (the line) by up to 0.2pp. Alcoholic drinks and tobacco, clothing and footwear, durable goods and miscellaneous goods share one colour; the tooltip shows each.
  6. Chart, seasonally adjusted: C&SD’s month-on-month change in the seasonally adjusted CPI and its food component (bars), with the seasonally adjusted index (line), for the 36 months in the latest Monthly Report. C&SD publishes seasonally adjusted indices for all items and food only.
  7. Chart, by household expenditure group: CPI(A), CPI(B) and CPI(C) cover about 50%, 30% and 10% of households, in the relatively low, medium and relatively high expenditure ranges; the CPI (C&SD’s Composite CPI) covers all three.
  8. Food and energy, housing: C&SD’s section and group indices. Food and non-alcoholic beverages (COICOP 01, from 2005) is C&SD’s own division on the international classification (table 510-60003): it excludes meals out and takeaway food, which COICOP puts under restaurants, and is the food measure used across the Asia dashboards. C&SD’s food section includes meals out. C&SD publishes no energy aggregate: Energy (calculated, from October 2020) combines its electricity, towngas, LPG and other fuel, and motor fuel groups at C&SD’s weights; see Methodology. Some group figures are missing in individual months of 2020–2023, when C&SD could not collect reliable prices during COVID-19.
  9. By COICOP division: C&SD’s CPI on the international COICOP 2018 classification (13 divisions), latest month.
  10. Largest price changes by group: the ten groups with the largest y/y increases and the ten with the largest falls in the latest month, out of C&SD’s 75 groups, with each group’s 2025 weight in the tooltip. These are price changes, not contributions.

Import and export prices: unit value indices of imports and total exports (2024 = 100), y/y. Unit value indices reflect changes in the mix of goods as well as in prices.

Consumer and producer prices

Hong Kong’s consumer price indices are compiled monthly by the Census and Statistics Department (C&SD) from over 40,000 price quotations a month from about 3,500 retail outlets and service providers, and from online outlets used by Hong Kong households. Prices are those consumers pay, including taxes and service charges and taking account of most discounts, subsidies and rebates.

Four indices

C&SD compiles CPI(A), CPI(B) and CPI(C) on the expenditure patterns of households in the relatively low, medium and relatively high expenditure ranges (about 50%, 30% and 10% of households), and the Composite CPI on the combined pattern of all three; this page calls the Composite CPI simply the CPI. Its year-on-year change is the usual measure of consumer price inflation. All four are on an October 2019 – September 2020 = 100 base. C&SD also publishes underlying indices, which net out the effects of the Government’s one-off relief measures since 2007.

Weights

Until 2020, C&SD updated the expenditure weights every five years from its Household Expenditure Survey (HES). Since 2021 it reviews them every year, using retail sales, restaurant receipts and national accounts data, and applies the new weights (with the previous year as reference period) from the April reference month. The 2024/25 HES was used to revise the 2025 weights, applied from April 2026, together with a revised classification of 75 commodity/service groups (88 before). The page shows the 2025 weights for the CPI as published in Annex 1 to the Monthly Report on the CPI.

  • 2025 weights, CPI (% of CPI): food 25.27 (meals out and takeaway food 15.92, basic food 9.35); housing 39.69; electricity, gas and water 2.80; alcoholic drinks and tobacco 0.41; clothing and footwear 2.10; durable goods 3.44; miscellaneous goods 3.13; transport 7.59; miscellaneous services 15.57.

Energy CPI (calculated)

C&SD publishes no energy index. The page calculates one on the definition used across the Asia dashboards (household energy plus fuels for personal transport; water excluded; COICOP 2018 04.5 + 07.2.2): C&SD’s groups electricity; towngas; liquefied petroleum gas and other fuel; and fuels for personal transport equipment (motor fuel), weighted by C&SD’s published expenditure weights for the CPI (Annex 1 to the Monthly Report on the CPI). Nothing else is estimated.

  • Weights (% of CPI): 2019/20 weights (used October 2019 – March 2024): electricity 1.78, towngas 0.66, LPG and other fuel 0.12, motor fuel 0.56 (total 3.12); 2023 (April 2024 – March 2025): 2.15, 0.55, 0.11, 0.55 (3.36); 2024 (April 2025 – March 2026): 2.04, 0.55, 0.12, 0.50 (3.21); 2025 (from April 2026): 1.83, 0.61, 0.10, 0.43 (2.97).
  • Method: as C&SD compiles its own aggregates. On the 2019/20 weights the groups are on the October 2019 – September 2020 = 100 base and are averaged at those weights; each later set of weights is chained in at March, the month before it applies. y/y is calculated from that index, so it starts in October 2020.
  • Check: the same method applied to electricity, towngas, LPG and water reproduces C&SD’s published section index “Electricity, gas and water” within 0.1 index points every month since October 2019 (the difference is rounding of the published one-decimal indices). The pipeline stops if it exceeds 0.15.
  • Relief measures: the index is on the headline CPI and so includes the Government’s electricity charges subsidies, which moved electricity prices sharply in 2020–21 and in later years. C&SD publishes underlying indices only for sections, not for groups, so an underlying energy index cannot be built from published figures.

CPI less food and non-alcoholic beverages and energy (calculated)

C&SD publishes no core measure (its underlying CPI removes only the Government’s one-off relief measures). The page calculates the core measure used for India and Singapore and on the Asia tab: the CPI less food and non-alcoholic beverages (COICOP 01; meals out and takeaway food stay in) and less energy (as defined above).

  • Food and non-alcoholic beverages: C&SD’s basic food (groups 2–22 on the 2025 classification), the only food aggregate with a published weight. Its y/y is within 0.1pp of C&SD’s COICOP 01 division every month since October 2005.
  • Weights (% of CPI): basic food 10.36 (2019/20 weights), 9.10 (2023), 8.71 (2024), 9.35 (2025); energy as above. The measure covers 86.5% of the CPI on the 2019/20 weights and 87.7% on the 2025 weights.
  • Method: the nine sections at C&SD’s published weights, less basic food and the four energy groups at theirs, on the 2019/20 base and chained in at March for each later set of weights, as for energy. y/y from that index, from October 2020.
  • Check: the nine sections at their weights reproduce C&SD’s published all-items CPI within 0.09 index points every month since October 2019 (rounding); the pipeline stops above 0.15.
  • Relief measures: on the headline CPI, so it includes the Government’s rates concessions and public housing rent waivers (housing is in the measure); electricity subsidies fall out with energy.

What C&SD publishes

  • Indices and rates: index, m/m and y/y for all items and the nine sections (from 1980), the 75 groups (Table E501), and the CPI by COICOP 2018 division (from 2005).
  • Contributions: the contribution of each section to CPI y/y in the reference month, in the Monthly Report on the CPI.
  • Seasonally adjusted: the average monthly change over the latest three months (full history for the headline indices; 36 months for the underlying), and the seasonally adjusted index and m/m for all items and food (36 months per report).
  • Not published: a goods/services split, an energy index and a CPI excluding food and energy (the page calculates both; above), item-level indices.

Producer and trade prices

C&SD compiles producer price indices for manufacturing and for selected service industries quarterly (2015 = 100). There is no monthly PPI. Its monthly merchandise trade index numbers include unit value indices of imports and total exports (2024 = 100), which move with prices and with the mix of goods traded.

Releases

C&SD releases the CPI at 16:30 Hong Kong time (16:30 Singapore), around the 20th to 23rd of the following month, on the dates in its published schedule; the data tables and the Monthly Report are updated at the same time. Quarterly PPI is released about ten weeks after the quarter; trade unit value indices about six weeks after the month. The page is refreshed the day after each release.

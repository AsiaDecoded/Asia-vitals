Core 3 (less food and energy)

Core 3 is an Asia Decoded measure of underlying inflation, built so Japan can be compared with core inflation in India and other economies that keep restaurant meals in their core measure.

Why it is needed

Japan publishes three core-type measures. Core 1 removes fresh food only, and Core 2 removes fresh food and energy. The third, “all items less food (excluding alcoholic beverages) and energy”, removes all of the Food group. In Japan’s CPI, eating out sits inside Food, so that measure drops restaurant meals too. India’s core measure (CPI excluding food and beverages and fuel) keeps them, because restaurant meals are a separate category there.

Definition

  • Excluded: food (including non-alcoholic drinks), electricity, gas, kerosene and gasoline.
  • Kept: eating out (including school lunches, which international classifications treat as canteen meals), alcoholic drinks, and everything outside food and energy.

Calculation

Japan’s CPI is a fixed-weight index, so within one base an aggregate equals the weighted average of its parts. Core 3 is the weighted average of two published indices, “all items less food (excl. alcoholic beverages) and energy” and “eating out”, using the Statistics Bureau’s published weights for each base (from Table 5 of the monthly report). Within a base this is the index the Bureau would publish if it defined the aggregate.

The year-on-year rate combines the two parts’ published year-on-year rates, each weighted by its weight times its index a year earlier (the aggregation rule for a fixed-weight index). Using the published rates, which the Bureau calculates from unrounded indices, avoids the rounding in the one-decimal indices; the result is checked against the change in the combined index.

Everything is on the base the Bureau uses for that month’s official rates: the 2015 base for 2016 to 2020, the 2020 base for 2021 to 2025 and the 2025 base from 2026. Both months of every comparison are on the same base. Data come from the e-Stat database for each base, national, monthly. As a check, the same calculation applied to the published “less food and energy” index alone reproduces its published year-on-year rate.

Limits

  • Not an official series: the Statistics Bureau does not publish this aggregate.
  • From 2016: earlier years would need indices from older bases.
  • National only: there is no Tokyo version, and no seasonally adjusted series, so it appears only as a 12-month rate in the momentum table.
  • Definitions still differ: energy and the edges of food are defined slightly differently in each country’s CPI, so the measures are comparable, not identical.

Food and non-alcoholic beverages

The food measure follows the international standard (COICOP division 01, food and non-alcoholic beverages), so Japan can be compared with India and other economies.

Why it is needed

Japan’s Food group also contains alcoholic beverages and eating out (including school lunches). COICOP puts these in separate divisions (alcohol with tobacco; restaurants and canteens with accommodation). Japan does not publish a food and non-alcoholic beverages aggregate.

Definition

  • Included: fresh food, meat, rice and other cereals, dairy and eggs, processed and prepared food bought from shops, and non-alcoholic drinks (tea, coffee, soft drinks, juices).
  • Excluded: alcoholic drinks, and eating out (restaurants, takeaway, school lunches). Pet food and tobacco are outside Japan’s Food group already.

Calculation

Food and non-alcoholic beverages is the published Food index less alcoholic beverages and eating out, using the Statistics Bureau’s published weights for each base. Within a base, Japan’s fixed-weight index makes this exact. The year-on-year rate combines the three parts’ published rates, each weighted by its weight times its index a year earlier, and is checked against the change in the combined index. It uses the base the Bureau uses for that month’s official rates: 2010 base for 2011 to 2015, 2015 base for 2016 to 2020, 2020 base for 2021 to 2025, and 2025 base from 2026.

Energy

Energy is the Statistics Bureau’s published series, which matches the international definition: electricity, city and LP gas, kerosene and gasoline. Japan’s CPI has no diesel item.

Limits

  • Not an official series: the Statistics Bureau does not publish this aggregate.
  • Rounding: because parts are subtracted, rounding in the published inputs can move the rate by up to about 0.1 percentage point.
  • From 2011, national only.

CPI diffusion index

The diffusion index measures how broad-based inflation is: not how fast prices are rising on average, but how many of the things people buy are getting more expensive.

Definition

For each month, every individual item in Japan’s national CPI is classified by its year-on-year price change:

  • Rising: y/y above zero
  • Falling: y/y below zero
  • Unchanged: y/y exactly zero

The chart shows the share of items rising and the share falling, as a percentage of all items counted, and the diffusion index: share rising minus share falling. It runs from −100 (every item falling) to +100 (every item rising). Zero means as many items are rising as falling.

Data

  • Source: Statistics Bureau of Japan, monthly CPI report, Table 5 (item indices and contributions, national and Tokyo), one Excel file per month on e-Stat, July 2016 onward.
  • Y/y changes: the published rate for each item, taken from that month’s own report on the base in use at the time (2015, 2020 or 2025). No index values are recalculated.
  • Items: individual items only (the lowest level of the classification, such as a specific rice variety or mobile phone charges). Category totals such as food or goods are excluded, so nothing is counted twice.
  • Inclusion: an item is counted in a month if it has a weight and a published y/y for that month.

Why the number of items varies

  • Seasonal items: some items, such as certain fresh fruit and seasonal clothing, are priced only in season and drop out in other months.
  • Rebasing: each five-yearly rebasing adds and drops items. Items new to a base have no y/y until they have been priced for a year.

Choices and limits

  • Unweighted: each item counts once, whatever its weight. A small item counts as much as rent or electricity. This is deliberate: the index is about breadth, and the weighted picture is in the CPI itself and the contributions chart.
  • Fresh food included: the Bank of Japan’s diffusion index excludes fresh food. This one includes all items, so it is somewhat more volatile and will not match the Bank’s figures exactly.
  • Threshold of zero: an item rising 0.1% counts the same as one rising 20%. A small change in many items can move the index a lot.
  • Base splices: the item basket changes at each rebasing (2021 and 2026 in this period), so small level shifts around those dates partly reflect the change of basket.

Production

The asia-vitals data pipeline finds each month’s Table 5 file through the e-Stat file catalogue, keeps an unchanged copy, reads every item’s weight and y/y, and computes the shares and the index. Older files use a different layout without item codes; there, items are identified as the lowest rows of the classification. The pipeline checks the result each month (at least 400 items counted) before the dashboard is updated.

NBS publishes no index levels and no seasonally adjusted series.

Consumer and producer prices

China’s consumer price index (CPI) and producer price indices are compiled monthly by the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS). The CPI covers the goods and services bought by urban and rural households in 31 provinces, about 120,000 outlets in about 500 cities and counties, eight categories and 268 basic categories. The producer price index for industrial products (PPI) covers ex-factory prices of more than 1,300 basic categories in 41 industries; the purchasing price index for industrial producers covers more than 800 basic categories of inputs.

What NBS publishes

NBS publishes rates only: indices with the comparison period = 100, to one decimal, for the same month a year earlier (y/y), the same period of the previous year (year to date) and the previous month (m/m). There are no index levels, no seasonally adjusted series and no item-level indices. The page shows these as published, with 100.9 written as 0.9%, the figure in NBS’s release.

  • CPI: all items for China, urban and rural areas; food, non-food, consumer goods, services and CPI excluding food and energy (China only); the eight categories (China, urban, rural, each province) and about 30 subcategories and food items (China).
  • PPI: ex-factory prices with means of production and consumer goods; purchasing prices with nine groups; ex-factory prices by industry; PPI and purchasing prices by province.
  • Weights: 2025-base weights for the eight categories, food and four food items (NBS, 11 February 2026): food, tobacco, liquor and dining out 29.5%, of which food 17.2% (grain 1.2%, fresh vegetables 1.7%, pork 1.9%, fresh fruits 1.9%); clothing 5.4%; residence 22.1%; household facilities, articles and services 5.5%; transportation and telecommunication 14.3%; education, culture and recreation 11.4%; health care and medical services 8.9%; miscellaneous goods and services 2.9%. NBS notes these are base-period weights: the weights behind each y/y and m/m rate shift with relative prices.
  • Impacts on CPI: NBS states in the text of its monthly release and interpretation how much some items moved the CPI, in percentage points. These are shown as stated; they are not a complete breakdown.

Base periods and classification

NBS rebases the CPI and PPI every five years. Since January 2026 both are on a 2025 base, with a revised basket, outlets and weights; NBS estimates the effect on y/y rates at 0.06pp for the CPI and 0.08pp for the PPI on average. Earlier bases started in January 2001, 2006, 2011, 2016 and 2021. In January 2016 the CPI categories changed from the old eight (food; tobacco, liquor and articles; clothing; household facilities; health care and personal articles; transport and communication; recreation, education and culture; residence) to the current eight; the old categories are shown to December 2015. In January 2026 “food, tobacco and liquor” became “food, tobacco, liquor and dining out” and “tourism” became “travel agencies and other touring services”. The portal publishes each base period’s categories as separate tables; the page joins them with no adjustment, and the data mark each base change.

Releases

NBS releases the CPI and PPI together at 09:30 Beijing time (09:30 Singapore), usually on the 9th to 15th of the following month, on the dates in its published release calendar. The figures are not revised.

Data

NBS data portal (data.stats.gov.cn, rebuilt in 2025–26; its JSON interface is open, with no key), monthly national and provincial price tables; NBS monthly releases and interpretations (stats.gov.cn) for the impacts; 2025-base weights from NBS’s interview on the base rotation (11 February 2026). Built by the AsiaDecoded/asia-vitals pipeline (data/cn/cpi, data/cn/ppi), which fetches only on NBS release days.

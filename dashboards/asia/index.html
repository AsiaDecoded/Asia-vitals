Food & bev is food and non-alcoholic beverages (COICOP 01). Core is CPI excluding food and non-alcoholic beverages and energy. Official core is the measure the statistics office or central bank publishes. Blank where an economy publishes no such series or its build is not merged yet. Series by economy: Methodology.

Official core by economy. Japan: CPI less fresh food (Statistics Bureau). South Korea: CPI excluding food and energy (Bank of Korea's core measure). Taiwan: CPI excluding fruit, vegetables and energy (DGBAS). Hong Kong: underlying Composite CPI, netting out one-off government relief measures (C&SD). China: CPI excluding food and energy (NBS). India: none published. Indonesia: core, excluding volatile food and administered prices (BPS). Thailand: core, excluding fresh food and energy (TPSO). Malaysia: core, excluding the most volatile items and administered prices (DOSM). Singapore: MAS core, CPI excluding accommodation and private transport. Philippines: core, excluding rice, corn, fresh meat, fresh fish, fruit, vegetables, electricity, LPG, diesel and gasoline (PSA). Vietnam: core, excluding food and foodstuffs, energy and state-managed prices including health and education services (NSO).

Source: national statistics offices, via each economy's dashboard.

GNI per capita since becoming middle income

Economies

Index, GNI per capita (Atlas method, US$) = 100 in year t, the year each economy became lower-middle income. Horizontal axis: years from t.

t is the first data year the World Bank classified the economy lower-middle income after low income (China 1997, India 2007, Indonesia 1993, the first of two crossings, Vietnam 2009). Thailand, Malaysia and the Philippines were already lower-middle income when the classifications began, so t = 1987, the first year. Index calculated by Asia Decoded. Taiwan is not in the World Bank’s data; Japan, South Korea, Hong Kong and Singapore were upper-middle or high income from 1987.

Source: World Bank, World Development Indicators (NY.GNP.PCAP.CD) and historical income classifications.

What the table shows

Each figure is copied from the economy's own dashboard data. This page calculates nothing new from it, apart from the summary columns you asked for: the 2015–19 average, the lowest and highest y/y since January 2021 (the first month when a value repeats) and the last 24 months of CPI y/y. "Calculated" below means the series is calculated in that economy's own pipeline (set out on its dashboard's Methodology tab).

EconomyColumnSeriesBasisFromLatest

GDP tab: GNI per capita, Atlas method, current US$, from the World Bank’s World Development Indicators, as published, indexed to 100 in year t (calculated). t is the first data year the World Bank’s historical classifications put the economy in the lower-middle income group after the low income group (the data year, not the July in which the classification was announced; Indonesia’s first crossing, 1993, before it fell back to low income after the Asian financial crisis). Economies already lower-middle income when the classifications began take that first year, 1987.

Financial tab: each currency’s year-to-date change is calculated on this page’s data, at your request: the BIS daily rate (national currency per US dollar, from national sources) on the last trading day of the previous year divided by the latest rate, minus 1, in %. That is the change in the currency’s US-dollar value, so a positive figure means the currency has strengthened. Each economy uses its own latest date. Vietnam is left out: the BIS has no daily series for the dong.

Financial tab, stock markets: the benchmark index of each economy, daily closes from Yahoo Finance (not an official source), completed sessions only. Year-to-date change, calculated: latest close divided by the last close on or before 31 December of the previous year, minus 1, in %. Price index in local currency, so neither dividends nor currency moves are included. Thailand, the Philippines and Vietnam are left out because Yahoo Finance returns only the latest quote for their indices, with no history.

Food & bev is left blank for China (NBS food excludes beverages), Indonesia (the BPS group includes tobacco) and Thailand (TPSO food includes prepared food). Korea's official core is the Bank of Korea's measure, CPI excluding food and energy, the same series as its core.

Chart notes

  • Chart 1, GDP in US dollars: annual, from 1960, as published by the World Bank (World Development Indicators). Real: GDP at constant 2015 US dollars (NY.GDP.MKTP.KD). Nominal: GDP at current US dollars (NY.GDP.MKTP.CD), converted at the World Bank’s annual average exchange rates, so it moves with the peso as well as with output; with gross national income, Atlas method, current US$ (NY.GNP.ATLS.CD), the total behind Chart 2’s GNI per capita. US$ billion (the World Bank’s US$ divided by 109). Income groups (dashed lines): the Philippines was lower-middle income when the World Bank’s classifications began (1987 data, FY89) and became upper-middle income on its 2025 GNI per capita (FY27, announced July 2026). CAGR (Charts 1 and 2): compound annual growth from the World Bank figures, (end-year value / 1986 value)1/years − 1, for 1986 to the latest WDI year (2025 now) and 1986–2019 (before the pandemic); calculated on this page.
  • Chart 2, GDP per capita in US dollars: as Chart 1, in US$ per person: constant 2015 US$ (NY.GDP.PCAP.KD) and current US$ (NY.GDP.PCAP.CD). Nominal also shows GNI per capita, Atlas method (NY.GNP.PCAP.CD), the series behind the World Bank’s income classification, from the Asia dashboard’s central GNI dataset; it converts at a three-year average of exchange rates adjusted for inflation.
  • Chart 3, GDP growth: against the same quarter a year earlier, from the first quarter of 1982, as published by the PSA. Real: constant 2018 prices. Nominal: current prices. The PSA’s quarterly series is on one base throughout (its 2018-based series, carried back to 1981), so there are no breaks.
  • Chart 4, by sector: the PSA’s three major sectors, real, y/y, from 1982: agriculture, forestry and fishing; industry (mining and quarrying, manufacturing, electricity, steam, water and waste management, and construction); and services. The three sum to GDP; the PSA’s GDP has no separate taxes line.
  • Chart 5, annual growth by source: real GDP, % change on the previous year. PSA: its annual series at constant 2018 prices, from 1947. World Bank: World Development Indicators, from 1961 (equal to the PSA’s rates where both exist, to one decimal). The open circle labelled YTD is the current year so far, calculated by Asia Decoded from the PSA’s published quarterly levels at 2018 prices (the year’s quarters to date against the same quarters a year earlier); the PSA states the figure in its release text, not in its tables. Shown until the PSA publishes the full year.
  • Chart 6, annual growth by sector: the PSA’s annual real growth for GDP and the three major sectors, constant 2018 prices, from 1947 (agriculture from 1948); YTD as in Chart 5.

Data

Every figure on this page is published by the Philippine Statistics Authority and taken from its OpenSTAT database, except energy and CPI excluding food and energy, which are calculated (below). Nothing is linked or seasonally adjusted here.

Consumer prices

  • Index: CPI for all income households, 2018 = 100, classified by COICOP 2018 (13 divisions down to 4-digit classes). The current series starts in January 2018; for earlier years the PSA publishes a backcast on the same base: by commodity group from January 1994, all items from January 1957.
  • Year-on-year rates: the PSA’s published rates, January 2019 on from the current tables, January 1995 to December 2018 from the backcast tables, and 1958 to 1994 for all items.
  • Weights: the PSA’s published 2018 weights, shown as % of the national CPI basket. NCR and areas outside NCR weights are their shares of the national basket (about a fifth and four-fifths).
  • Regions: NCR, areas outside NCR and the 17 regions, all items, as published.

Core CPI

The PSA’s core CPI excludes rice, corn, fresh meat, fresh fish, fruits, vegetables, electricity, LPG, diesel and gasoline, see the core CPI weight in the division table. It is the PSA’s own definition, not a standard excluding all food and energy. The PSA publishes core y/y from January 2019 on the current tables and to December 2017 on the backcast, but none for 2018, so the core line has a gap that year.

Energy (calculated)

The PSA publishes no energy index. This one combines two published groups at the PSA’s published 2018 weights: electricity, gas and other fuels (COICOP 04.5: electricity, LPG, kerosene, wood and charcoal) and fuels and lubricants for personal transport (07.2.2: diesel, gasoline, other fuels, lubricants). Water is excluded. Its weight is shown in the division table.

  • Formula: the PSA CPI is a fixed-base (Laspeyres) index on 2018 = 100, so a group is the weighted average of its parts. Energy = (weight of 04.5 × index of 04.5 + weight of 07.2.2 × index of 07.2.2) ÷ the sum of the two weights; y/y is calculated from that index and rounded to one decimal.
  • Check: the same method reproduces the PSA’s published indices for 04.5, 07.2, division 04 and division 07 from their parts within 0.2 index points in every month since January 2018 (indices are published to one decimal). The pipeline stops if it does not.
  • Period: index from January 2018, y/y from January 2019. The PSA’s backcast before 2018 does not add up from its parts this way, so the series is not extended back.

CPI excluding food and energy (calculated)

CPI less food and non-alcoholic beverages (division 01) and energy (as defined above), for comparison with other economies. The PSA’s own core measure excludes a different, narrower set of items and is shown separately.

  • Formula: the weighted average, at the PSA’s published 2018 weights, of every published part outside food and energy: divisions 02, 03, 05, 06 and 08–13, the housing groups other than 04.5, and the transport groups and classes other than 07.2.2. Its weight is shown in the division table. y/y is calculated from that index and rounded to one decimal.
  • Check: the index matches the published all-items index with food and energy taken out at their weights, within 0.3 index points in every month since January 2018 (the gap comes from rounding in the published indices). The pipeline stops if it does not.
  • Period: index from January 2018, y/y from January 2019, for the same reason as energy.

Seasonally adjusted CPI

The PSA publishes a seasonally adjusted CPI and its month-on-month change for all items, food and non-alcoholic beverages, non-food and the divisions, from January 2018, for the Philippines, NCR and areas outside NCR. The momentum chart and the m/m column show those published rates.

Bottom 30% income households

A separate PSA index (2018 = 100) for households in the bottom 30% of the income distribution, January 2018 on, with y/y from January 2019. Food is about half of its basket, against about a third for all households.

Producer prices

PSA Producer Price Index for manufacturing (2018 = 100), from the Monthly Integrated Survey of Selected Industries, published about two months after the reference month. y/y as published from January 2019, and from the PSA’s backcast for total manufacturing for 2002–2017. No y/y is published for 2018.

Not on this page

Apart from energy and CPI excluding food and energy, the PSA publishes no goods and services split, no contributions to y/y in its database (only in the monthly press release) and no diffusion measure, so the page has none. Each could be calculated from the published indices and weights.

Exchange rate

The Financial tab shows peso per US dollar, daily, from the BIS bilateral exchange rate series (WS_XRU), compiled from national sources, as published, with nothing calculated. Full history from 1978. A higher number means a weaker peso.

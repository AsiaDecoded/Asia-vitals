  2. Data

Every figure on this page is published by BPS-Statistics Indonesia. Nothing is calculated, linked or seasonally adjusted here.

Where it comes from

  • BPS WebAPI, monthly CPI press release (Berita Resmi Statistik): Table 1 (CPI and the 11 expenditure groups) and the components table (core, administered prices, volatile food, energy, food): index, m/m, year to date, y/y and contributions to m/m and y/y, from January 2020. A month is kept only where the published rates agree with the published indices in the same table.
  • BPS WebAPI, static table 908: m/m for CPI, core, administered prices and volatile food from January 2009, used for the months before January 2020.
  • BPS WebAPI, monthly WPI press release: Table 1, the general wholesale price index (IHPB) and its five sections, 2023 = 100: index, m/m, year to date, y/y and contributions, from January 2025.
  • Bank Indonesia, SEKI table VIII.1: BPS’s CPI republished unchanged: all items from 1966 (long-run y/y, and the monthly chart’s m/m and y/y).

Consumer prices

  • Index: CPI, 2022 = 100, covering 150 cities, from January 2024. Earlier bases: 2018 = 100 (90 cities) 2020–2023, 2012 = 100 (82 cities) 2014–2019, 2007 = 100 (66 cities) June 2008–2013, 2002 = 100 (45 cities) 2004–May 2008, and older bases before. Index levels are not comparable across bases and are not linked here.
  • Expenditure groups: the 11 groups of COICOP 2018, used since January 2020.

Core inflation and components

BPS splits the CPI into three components: core, administered prices (goods and services whose prices the government sets, such as subsidised fuel, electricity tariffs and transport fares) and volatile food (food whose prices swing with supply, such as rice, chillies, shallots and poultry). The page shows BPS’s core measure only. BPS also publishes energy and food (bahan makanan) groupings, which overlap with the three components.

Seasonal adjustment

Neither BPS nor Bank Indonesia publishes a seasonally adjusted CPI, so the page has no seasonally adjusted m/m measure. Monthly changes carry strong seasonal patterns, especially around Ramadan and Eid.

Goods and services

BPS publishes no split of the CPI into goods and services, so the page has none.

Release timing

BPS releases the CPI and WPI on the first working day of the following month, at 11:00 Jakarta time (12:00 Singapore). Bank Indonesia adds the CPI to SEKI some days later.

Source: BPS-Statistics Indonesia, Bank IndonesiaAsia Decoded

GDP

Quarterly GDP from BPS’s national accounts, 2010 series (2010 constant prices), from Q1 2010, as published in the BPS WebAPI. Nothing is calculated here.

What is shown

  • Levels: Rp billion at 2010 prices and at current prices, by expenditure (household consumption and its seven groups, NPISH consumption, government consumption, fixed investment by asset, change in inventories, exports and imports of goods and services, statistical discrepancy) and by industry (17 categories of KBLI 2009 and their sub-industries).
  • Growth: BPS’s published rates at 2010 prices: q/q, y/y and year to date against the same period a year earlier (c-to-c).
  • Deflator: BPS’s implicit deflator growth (laju implisit), q/q and y/y.
  • Contributions: BPS’s sources of growth (sumber pertumbuhan), ppt of GDP growth, q/q, y/y and c-to-c. Imports are published as their own contribution, positive when imports grow, to be subtracted.

Seasonal adjustment

BPS publishes no seasonally adjusted GDP, so there are no annualised rates. q/q changes follow a strong seasonal pattern; y/y is BPS’s headline measure.

Revisions and history

BPS marks the latest three years as preliminary and revises them; each update replaces the whole series. Earlier series (2000 base, 1993 and older) are on different bases and are not linked here.

Release timing

About five weeks after the quarter (early February, May, August and November), 11:00 Jakarta time.

Source: BPS-Statistics IndonesiaAsia Decoded

Fiscal

Central government finance (the state budget, APBN) and government securities, from the Ministry of Finance as republished by Bank Indonesia in SEKI chapter IV. Nothing is calculated here.

What is shown

  • Budget and actual (SEKI IV.1–IV.3): annual revenue and grants, expenditure and financing, Rp billion, both the budget (APBN) and the outturn (realisasi), 2001 on (2001–2007 from SEKI’s older sheet; where it words an item differently, e.g. “PPH”, it is matched to the current sheet’s item). The 2004 budget surplus/deficit is taken from the revised 2004 budget law (UU 35/2004, APBN-P 2004, Article 12(1): a deficit of Rp 26.3 trillion), since SEKI shows +10.5 trillion where its own revenue less expenditure gives −26.3 trillion. Revenue by tax type and non-tax revenue; expenditure by economic type (personnel, goods, capital, interest, subsidies, grants, social assistance, other) plus transfers to regions and the village fund; financing by instrument.
  • Government securities (SEKI IV.4): tradable securities outstanding at month end, Rp billion: government bonds and treasury bills (SUN) by type and holder, and Islamic securities (SBSN) by type.

Not shown

  • Ratios to GDP: the % of GDP views of charts 1 and 3, and chart 4, divide SEKI’s revenue, expenditure and balance by nominal GDP (calculated): BPS’s annual GDP from 2010, the IMF World Economic Outlook’s (NGDP, linked by the IMF to BPS’s 2010 base) for 2001–2009. Debt and securities are not shown as % of GDP.
  • IMF general government balance (chart 2): World Economic Outlook, GGXCNL_NGDP, as published, from the IMF’s data API; updated with each WEO (April and October).
  • Monthly budget realisation: read from the Ministry’s monthly APBN KiTa press-conference deck (revenue, expenditure, surplus or deficit and primary balance, year to date, 2025 on); only the latest month is on the page for now, as the current year’s point in chart 1.
  • Total government debt: SBN excludes loans; the debt office’s debt profile is not read yet.
  • Years before 2001: SEKI keeps 1990–2000 on an older sheet (fiscal years April to March; 2000 April–December); only the totals are read, for chart 5.

Release timing

Bank Indonesia updates SEKI several weeks after the Ministry publishes; the budget tables change once or twice a year (new budget, then outturn), the securities table monthly.

Source: Ministry of Finance, Bank Indonesia (SEKI)Asia Decoded

Trade

Merchandise trade and trade price indices from BPS, as published in the BPS WebAPI. Nothing is calculated here.

What is shown

  • Values: monthly exports (FOB) and imports (CIF), US$ million, from customs documents (Directorate General of Customs and Excise), from January 2017: totals, trade balance, oil and gas and non-oil and gas, the ten SITC sections (from January 2022) and imports by use (consumption goods, raw materials and auxiliary goods, capital goods).
  • Prices: the International Trade Price Index (IHPI): export and import price indices, 2023 = 100, quarterly from Q1 2024, total, oil and gas and non-oil and gas, with BPS’s q/q and y/y. BPS moved them from 2010 = 100 in March 2025.

Not shown

Growth rates of export and import values: BPS states them in each release’s text and tables, not in its data tables, so the page has none yet. Unit value indices (monthly, by HS chapter) have no all-goods total and are left out.

Release timing

Monthly, with the CPI on the first working day of the second month after the reference month, 11:00 Jakarta time; trade price indices once a quarter with a monthly release.

Source: BPS-Statistics IndonesiaAsia Decoded
